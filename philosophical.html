A compass, not a ladder

Six movements of inner growth

A flexible path from noticing to care. These stages can overlap or repeat; they are prompts for exploration, not a measure of anyone's worth, a clinical model, or a proven universal law.

  1. 01 Awaken

    Notice what is here

    Story lens Creation begins with attention: light, life, relationship, and a world called good.

    Inner inquiry What do I notice before I rush to explain it?

    Discovery lens Practice observation; separate what you see from what you infer.

  2. 02 Attend

    Meet the unknown

    Story lens The wilderness stories hold uncertainty, need, and the search for direction.

    Inner inquiry What feeling or assumption might I be avoiding?

    Discovery lens Treat uncertainty as a reason to ask better questions, not to leap to certainty.

  3. 03 Discern

    Choose with wisdom

    Story lens Proverbs pictures wisdom as something sought, practiced, and tested in daily life.

    Inner inquiry Which values, evidence, and perspectives should shape this choice?

    Discovery lens Look for cognitive bias, check sources, and welcome thoughtful disagreement.

  4. 04 Transform

    Practice a new way

    Story lens Exodus can be read as a journey out of bondage toward responsibility and freedom.

    Inner inquiry What small, safe action would bring my choices closer to my values?

    Discovery lens Explore how habits and environments shape behavior; test changes gently.

  5. 05 Integrate

    Make meaning with care

    Story lens The Emmaus story makes room for grief, conversation, and seeing familiar things anew.

    Inner inquiry What have I learned, and what remains unresolved?

    Discovery lens Bring lived experience into dialogue with reliable research without treating one as proof of the other.

  6. 06 Contribute

    Turn insight toward life

    Story lens The Good Samaritan turns a question about goodness into a practice of neighborly care.

    Inner inquiry Who is affected by my choices, and whose voice is missing?

    Discovery lens Shape technology around human dignity, accessibility, accountability, and the common good.

Read each story in its literary and historical context. These psychological connections are reflective interpretations, not diagnoses or scientific claims.

Wonder and understanding

Faith, science & technology

Scripture invites spiritual and moral reflection. Science investigates the natural world through observation and evidence. Technology puts knowledge into practice—and asks us to consider how we use it.

We welcome thoughtful conversation between these areas without confusing spiritual belief with scientific proof. Curiosity, humility, and care can help us learn from questions that do not have easy answers.
